Doseday Capstone Submission

PROJECT THEME
DoseDay — an accessible medication companion for adults managing multiple prescriptions.

FIVE-SENTENCE PITCH
1. DoseDay is for Leila, a 58-year-old shift supervisor who manages four prescriptions for hypertension and type 2 diabetes.
2. She struggles to keep a consistent routine because doses are spread across changing workdays, side effects create doubt, and paper organizers are easy to misplace.
3. The product is a mobile-first medication companion that turns each dose into one calm daily action, supports quick check-ins, and shares a privacy-conscious weekly summary with a chosen caregiver.
4. In a four-week pilot with 30 adults managing three or more prescriptions, success means at least 80% of scheduled doses are marked “Taken” and the median first-time setup time is under three minutes.
5. I’m pursuing DoseDay because it combines accessible behavior design with a high-stakes daily habit, letting me build a useful, testable product rather than a one-off screen.

RESEARCH SYNTHESIS
- World Health Organization, 2003: adherence in developed countries averages about 50% for chronic-disease medicines.
  https://www.who.int/news/item/01-07-2003-failure-to-take-prescribed-medicine-for-chronic-diseases-is-a-massive-world-wide-problem
- CDC, 2017: barriers include complex dosing, side effects, cost, poor care coordination, and limited patient engagement.
  https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/grand-rounds/pp/2017/20170221-medication-adherence.html
- Older-adult medication-app review: common usability failures include difficult navigation, poor visibility, inflexible reminders, and too much manual entry.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5694345/
- Design response: simplify the daily view, use concrete labels with familiar icons, make check-in reversible, support flexible schedules, and never shame a missed dose.

PRIMARY USER
Leila, 58
Shift supervisor | Hypertension + type 2 diabetes | Four daily medications
Goals: take the right dose on time, understand side effects, preserve privacy, share only what she chooses.
Frustrations: changing shifts, overlapping reminders, tiny controls, uncertain refill timing, paper organizers that get lost.
Context: often checks her phone between work tasks; one-handed use; moderate vision and fine-motor needs.

CORE PROBLEM
People managing several prescriptions need one dependable daily routine, not another complicated health dashboard.

SUCCESS MEASUREMENT
Pilot: 30 adults, 4 weeks.
Primary target: at least 80% of scheduled doses marked Taken.
Setup target: median first-time setup under 3 minutes.
Guardrail: no dose decision overrides the prescription entered with the user or clinician.
