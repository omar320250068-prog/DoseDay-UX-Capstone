4. ASSUMPTIONS AND GAPS

Screener field reliability

Medication counts in the screener are given in the participant's own words in quotes and are corroborated in the body of each interview (see Section 1 table). Condition and age fields, however, are not always presented as direct quotes in the screener block (e.g., P1's "high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes," P3's "thyroid, cholesterol, blood pressure, bone, and the sugar one"). A product team should verify these condition labels directly with participants rather than treating them as clinically confirmed diagnoses, since it's unclear whether they are verbatim or paraphrased by the interviewer.

Single-participant claims embedded in composite archetypes

The "red circle" shaming reaction, and its emotional intensity, rests on P1 alone. No other participant describes having actually used and been penalized by a streak/red-flag mechanic in real time — P2's and P5's rejection of streaks is anticipatory/principled, not a report of a lived punitive event.
The "three states: now / later / already done" interaction model rests on P4 alone. It resonates conceptually with P2's "just a tap," but no other participant proposed this specific structure.
The desire for a personal, exportable log rests on P5 alone. P2, in the same merged archetype, explicitly wants the opposite. This should not be treated as a settled preference for "tech-fluent" users generally.
The "danger dot" idea (marking one medication as the risky one and separating it from the routine) rests on P3 alone.
Refill/stock-out stress as a major pain point rests on P1 and P4 only; P2, P3, and P5 do not mention pharmacy stock-outs at all. This should not be generalized as a universal need.

Unresolved contradictions requiring further research

Thyroid-medication timing rigidity: P5 insists on an exact time; P3, also on a thyroid medication, reports a flexible system with no mention of exact timing. It is unresolved whether this is individual variation, a difference in the specific medication, or an area P3 simply wasn't asked about.
Record-keeping preference within the "Tech-Fluent Minimalist" archetype: P2 wants zero record; P5 wants a full exportable log. A product team cannot default to either without further segmentation research.
The anomalous third-person line in P3's transcript ("She crosses a name off the whiteboard...") is unresolved and should be clarified with P3 directly before it informs any design decision about caregivers or shared routines.

Gaps not covered by any participant

No participant describes a scenario involving a change in dosage or a prescription change mid-routine — only steady-state regimens are represented.
No participant discusses cost or insurance as a barrier to refills; the refill pain points described (P1, P4) are about timing/logistics, not affordability.
Family-sharing preferences are only discussed by three of five participants (P1, P3, P4); P2 has no relevant family member in the picture, and P5 wants zero behavioral sharing. "Selective sharing" as a feature should be scoped as relevant to a subset of users, not a universal requirement.
No participant was asked directly what "as needed" guidance they'd want beyond dosage timing (e.g., maximum frequency safeguards) — this surfaced incidentally (P1's caution about the "strong" pain medication) but was not explored as a distinct research question with any participant.
