## Transcript

**INT:** You're on the phone with a laptop in front of you, so I'll start with the thing I'm most interested in: you clearly use a lot of apps. What have you got on your phone?

**P2:** Too many. I use about thirty apps. I review my health the way I review my finances. I have a watch, the phone, my banking, my calendar, my training app, my football app. I'm not precious about my phone. If an app is slow it goes.

**INT:** Have you got anything medication-related on there right now?

**P2:** I had one. I deleted it last year. And I had one before that, and I deleted that too, so I'm on my third attempt in four years and I've never kept one. That's the honest headline.

**INT:** Why the third deletion?

**P2:** Because it's a diary with a push notification attached. Everything I tried wants me to record things. There's a mood tracker, a water tracker, a weight, a note field, an "any side effects?" field. Why is it interested in my mood? I'm not here for my mood, I'm here for my blood pressure tablet at half seven in the morning.

**INT:** What's the minimum you actually need?

**P2:** A tap. That's it. A tap, and a schedule that survives a shift change. I don't need a diary.

**INT:** Is there anything you dislike that isn't about your medication?

**P2:** Sign-up. Every single one of them wants me to create an account, verify an email, pick a password, agree to something, and then ask for my date of birth. I have two hundred accounts. I'm not creating another account to swallow a pill. Give me a widget, put it on my lock screen, done. If I have to sign in, I won't do it tomorrow.

**INT:** What does your morning look like?

**P2:** I get up at 4:50. It's a warehouse — I start at 6, I'm the team lead, so I'm in before the team, I do the safety briefing. Breakfast in the car or nothing, depending. Tablet with the first coffee.

**INT:** And the second one?

**P2:** The second one is in the evening, with food, about half seven, and that's the one that's fragile. If I'm late out — and I'm late out maybe twice a week — that's the one that goes. I get home at nine, I eat, and then I think, do I really need this tonight? And honestly, the answer's sometimes no. Because the whole point of the evening one is that it's before dinner, and I missed the window.

**INT:** So how often?

**P2:** I'd say... hmm. Twice a week? Maybe. I never miss the morning one. I never miss the morning one. I can say that with confidence, because it's attached to the coffee. The evening one is different. I would not say I never miss a dose. I'd say the morning one is bulletproof and the evening one is a coin toss when I'm late.

**INT:** Would a dose log make you more or less honest?

**P2:** A dose log makes me a worse person. As soon as you're recording, red goes on the screen, and a red thing on a Tuesday evening when I've been on my feet for fourteen hours is — I'd rather it just didn't exist. I'd rather not have the app know. There's a difference between not doing it and being told you didn't do it.

**INT:** You keep a paper box too?

**P2:** A weekly box, Sunday night, seven compartments, and I put them in the order — the morning ones in the morning ones. It's a Sunday job, two minutes. I'd put my phone in a box before I did that on a Friday. The box has never let me down and I don't have to be anywhere near it.

**INT:** What about apps that want to be your companion? There's a wellness framing in a lot of them.

**P2:** I don't need to be a patient. I need a tap. Anything that frames me as a patient, or that says "we're proud of you," I'm gone. My blood pressure isn't a personality. I just want the thing to work and be out of the way.

**INT:** If an app did the tap perfectly, would you share it?

**P2:** If the tap was good and it didn't ask me anything, I'd put it on my lock screen. I'd recommend it. But if it has a streak — a streak is a weapon, by the way. A streak is just a counter that can be turned against you. I've had a 40-day streak and I ended it on purpose because I knew it was coming and I didn't want the drop. I'd rather have no streak at all than a broken one. That's a real insight from my own life, that.

**INT:** Side effects, at all?

**P2:** Nothing dramatic. The first week on the blood pressure one I got a bit dizzy standing up, and I read about it on a forum and it was normal. So I've been fine. If the app had told me that on day two I'd have stopped googling. That's a genuinely useful thing and nobody does it.

**INT:** Who else in your family takes anything?

**P2:** Nobody. I was going to say my mother, but no, she doesn't. It's just me. My partner thinks it's boring. If it were about her as well I'd be more careful, but it's just me, so the stakes are lower than people assume.

**INT:** What about driving home after the late shift? Anything about being alert?

**P2:** Not for these. I'm not on anything that makes me groggy, and I drive the van home at 6am, which is a different story but unrelated. Don't put that in the same bucket.

**INT:** Understood — keeping them separate.

**P2:** Good, yeah, keep those separate. People lump everything together.

**INT:** Last one. If a product team built the perfect version of this, what would you tell them to leave out?

**P2:** The feeding. The streaks, the badges, the "you're on a roll", the encouraging messages when I do what I'm supposed to. It's noise with a smiley face. And the report. I don't want a report. I don't want a chart of my own compliance. I already know.

**INT:** If you don't want a report, what do you want?

**P2:** Nothing. The best version of this product is the one I stop noticing. Leave it out and it works better.
